# 2026 May Be a Good Time to Invest in Nokia

When I first heard about **Nokia’s new partnership with NVIDIA**, I was honestly surprised. I had not thought much about Nokia in years. Like many people, I still remembered Nokia as the cellphone company from my childhood — the company that seemed to be everywhere, then slowly disappeared from the conversation.

But the more I learned, the more interesting the story became.

I remember Nokia from the days of the **Snake game**. As a kid, that was such a cool thing. If one of my friends’ parents had a Nokia phone, we would ask to play Snake and find out what their high score was. The graphics were simple and pixelated, but somehow the game was incredibly entertaining.

Those phones were reliable too. The battery seemed to last for days. Some people could text on them unbelievably fast using the number pad. Back then, **Nokia was the phone to have**. BlackBerry felt like the phone for executives. **Nokia was for the rest of us.**

I was a Nokia fan. Years later, when Nokia released its beautiful **Lumia Windows Phones**, I wanted one. I liked the design, and I wanted Nokia to make a comeback. But I was disappointed by the reviews of Windows Phone. It lacked too many apps, and by that point iPhone and Android had already taken over. The Nokia phone itself looked great. I just did not want Windows.

To this day, I think Nokia’s biggest mistake was choosing Windows Phone instead of Android. Had it gone with Android early enough, I wonder whether Nokia might still be one of the major phone makers today.

But what I recently learned is that **Nokia did not simply disappear after losing the phone market. It changed direction.**

In 2014, Nokia sold its phone business to Microsoft. Instead of continuing to fight a losing battle in smartphones, it focused more heavily on the less visible side of its business: **the technology that helps connect the world**. Today, Nokia is deeply involved in network infrastructure — the systems behind mobile networks, high-speed internet, data centers, and future communication technology.

That is what makes the NVIDIA partnership so interesting to me.

In October 2025, **NVIDIA announced a $1 billion investment in Nokia** as part of a partnership focused on the future of AI-powered mobile networks and the path from 5G toward 6G. Nokia and NVIDIA are working together on technology that could help make future networks faster, smarter, and better prepared for a world where AI is built into more of the devices and systems we use every day.

I do not think you need to understand every technical detail to see why this matters.

**AI is the future**, but AI does not exist in a vacuum. It needs chips, data centers, electricity, and strong networks capable of moving enormous amounts of information quickly. We are going to need better digital infrastructure as AI becomes more common in phones, cars, businesses, factories, and everyday life. Nokia is involved in building that kind of infrastructure.

And now one of the most important AI companies in the world is investing directly in Nokia.

That caught my attention.

It also seems that Nokia’s position is already improving. In April 2026, Reuters reported that Nokia’s first-quarter results were lifted by growing demand tied to AI and cloud infrastructure. Its sales to AI and cloud customers rose sharply, and its shares reached their highest level in 16 years after the earnings report.

Of course, that does not mean Nokia is guaranteed to become a huge winner. Investing always involves risk, and no partnership automatically guarantees long-term success. But personally, I think Nokia has a very compelling story right now.

It is a company many people still associate with the past, but it may be quietly building a place in the future.

Nokia was once known for putting a phone in almost everyone’s pocket. Now it is helping build the infrastructure that could support the next generation of AI-powered technology. That is a fascinating transformation.

For me, this is what makes Nokia worth paying attention to in 2026:

- It is a familiar company with a long history and a strong brand.
- It survived losing the phone market by reinventing itself.
- It is involved in critical network infrastructure that the modern world depends on.
- It is directly partnering with NVIDIA, one of the biggest names in AI.
- It may benefit from the continued growth of AI, cloud computing, and future wireless networks.

I do not know exactly what Nokia will become over the next decade. But I do think people may be underestimating it because they still see it as an old phone company.

I see something different.

I see a company that lost the business it was famous for, used that moment to rebuild around a quieter but important business, and now finds itself connected to one of the biggest technology trends in the world.

That is why I believe:

## **2026 may be a good time to invest in Nokia.**
